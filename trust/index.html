02 / HOSPITAL PILOT SCOPE

Administrative equipment management first.

The proposed initial pilot focuses on equipment inventory, asset identity, maintenance, calibration, fault reporting and administrative lifecycle records.

Staff account and activity information may still be personal information and requires appropriate privacy review.

Excluded from the initial pilot scope

Patient names, medical record numbers, patient-linked device readings, clinical records and patient-specific treatment decisions. Patient-identifiable clinical information is prohibited in initial pilots.

Equipment records must not be used as a substitute for physical inspection or clinical clearance.

03 / REGIONAL HOSPITAL REQUIREMENTS

Requirements depend on the hospital and location.

Each institution must review its intended use, data flows, policies and applicable requirements before any live pilot decision.

01 / US

United States

Pilot assessment with the hospital must address HIPAA applicability, relevant state privacy and cybersecurity requirements, FDA software intended-use considerations and institutional vendor security requirements. Where HIPAA applies, appropriate safeguards and agreements must be established before regulated processing.

02 / CA

Canada

Pilot assessment must address applicable PIPEDA and provincial privacy requirements, Health Canada software intended-use considerations, hospital policies, hosting and cross-border data processing. Data residency and regulatory status require separate verification.

03 / INTL

International hospitals

Outside the United States and Canada, institutional requirements, local law, processing locations, cross-border access and relevant agreements need a hospital-specific assessment before activation. No single global approval applies.

04 / NEXT STEPS

Your path to hospital participation.

Prospective hospitals may express interest and discuss a pilot. Synthetic demonstrations may be offered where their isolation is verified. A conversation is not authorization for live use.

  1. Define the use. Agree the administrative equipment workflow, data boundaries and responsible hospital contacts.
  2. Review the institution. Complete applicable agreements, privacy and security assessment, vendor review and hospital authorization.
  3. Verify readiness. Complete and verify the relevant readiness gates before any live hospital activation.

Do not submit patient information through the public site. Read our Privacy notice before sending an inquiry. Relevant security, privacy and pilot documentation can be discussed during institutional review.
