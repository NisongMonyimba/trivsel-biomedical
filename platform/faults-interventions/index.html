THE OPERATING PROBLEM

Problems become useful only when their context survives.

A fault report on its own says very little. Teams need to know which asset was affected, where it was, who acknowledged the issue, what intervention occurred, what evidence was recorded and whether the asset's current state changed afterward.

Asset anchored

Every problem remains tied to the identity of the physical asset it concerns.

Context retained

Location and operational context remain available while the issue moves through response.

Evidence preserved

Notes, actions and resolution evidence remain part of the asset's history.

History connected

Repeated issues and previous interventions can be reviewed in sequence instead of isolation.

FAULT RECORD ANATOMY

One event, multiple pieces of evidence.

Trivsel treats a fault as more than a status label. The record connects the issue to the surrounding operational facts that make the event understandable later.

What happened

What was done

The intervention, assessment, service activity or other recorded response.

04

What changed

The resolution and resulting operational state retained for future review.

PEOPLE IN THE LOOP

Different roles see the same event from different operational angles.

Operator, nurse or frontline staff

Reports the issue close to where it occurred and adds the first operational context.

Engineer or technician

Acknowledges the issue, records intervention work and documents the outcome.

Manager or administrator

Reviews unresolved issues, response history and the operational context around recurring problems.
