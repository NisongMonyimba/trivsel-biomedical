PLATFORM / MAINTENANCE

Maintenance should become part of the asset's memory.

Trivsel connects planned and corrective maintenance to the physical asset that received the work, so organizations can understand what was done, when it happened, why it happened and what the asset's history now says.

Explore calibration Discuss a pilot

WHY ORGANIZATIONS SHOULD CARE

Maintenance is not only a task. It is evidence about the condition, use and history of a physical asset.

A maintenance activity can answer important operational questions only when the organization can connect the work to the correct asset, its location, the problem or planned need, the person or team involved and the resulting state of the equipment.

Know what work was actually done

Keep the maintenance action connected to the asset instead of leaving service information in separate notes, messages or spreadsheets.

Keep planned work visible

Give teams a structured way to see maintenance that is due, planned, completed or still awaiting attention.

Build lifecycle evidence

Each completed maintenance event becomes part of the asset's history, helping future teams understand what has already happened.

See patterns over time

Connected maintenance history can make recurring interventions, repeated issues and service burden easier to review.

PLANNED + CORRECTIVE WORK

Different maintenance paths. One connected asset history.

Preventive work and corrective work begin for different reasons, but both become more useful when the resulting evidence stays attached to the same physical asset.

01

Preventive maintenance

Work planned in advance to inspect, service, adjust or replace items before a known maintenance need becomes overdue.

Schedule→Perform→Record→Next due state
02

Corrective maintenance

Work that responds to a fault, failed check, observed problem or other condition requiring an intervention.

Problem→Intervention→Resolution→History

ANATOMY OF A MAINTENANCE RECORD

The useful question is not just "Was maintenance done?"

The record becomes operationally meaningful when it can explain the asset, timing, maintenance reason, work performed, evidence and resulting condition in one connected context.

01

Asset

The persistent identity of the physical unit that received the work.

02

Maintenance context

Planned or corrective reason, date, location, responsible team and relevant notes.

03

Work performed

The inspection, repair, replacement, adjustment or other maintenance activity completed.

04

Outcome + evidence

Completion state, supporting evidence and the updated asset history after the work.

WHO CARES ABOUT MAINTENANCE?

The same maintenance history supports different decisions across the organization.

ENGINEERS & TECHNICIANS

See what happened before starting the next job

Review prior interventions, work history and asset context before repeating or escalating maintenance.

OPERATIONS

Understand service state around active assets

Know whether an asset is awaiting work, undergoing maintenance or returning to normal operation.

ASSET MANAGEMENT

Connect maintenance burden to lifecycle history

Keep service events tied to the asset when reviewing its ongoing use, transfer or replacement needs.

PROCUREMENT

Bring service history into replacement discussions

Review accumulated maintenance context when considering repair, replacement or standardization choices.

FINANCE

Reference the physical asset behind maintenance activity

Keep operational service discussions anchored to the same equipment unit that other teams recognize.

LEADERSHIP & QUALITY

Review whether maintenance evidence is traceable

Follow the sequence of planned work, corrective work and resulting asset history without rebuilding the story manually.

THE MAINTENANCE LOOP

Maintenance becomes more useful when each action feeds the next decision.

  1. 01Identify the asset

    Start with the exact physical unit that needs attention.

  2. 02Understand the need

    Determine whether the work is planned, fault-driven or otherwise required.

  3. 03Perform the work

    Record the intervention, service activity and responsible people.

  4. 04Attach evidence

    Keep completion details and relevant supporting information with the asset record.

  5. 05Update the asset story

    Carry the result into lifecycle history so the next decision starts with better context.

CONNECTED MAINTENANCE

Maintenance becomes operational intelligence when it remains connected to identity, context and history.

Trivsel treats maintenance as one part of the broader physical-asset operating model. The asset provides identity. Context explains where and how it is being used. Maintenance adds history. That history can then support better questions and better human decisions.

Asset→Context→Maintenance→History→Action
