Asset management fundamentals (10)

  • What is equipment management?

    Equipment management coordinates identification, location, condition, use, maintenance, calibration and retirement of equipment.

  • What is asset management?

    Asset management coordinates asset-related decisions to deliver value over the lifecycle. Equipment management is one practical application.

  • What is an equipment lifecycle?

    The equipment lifecycle spans need definition, acquisition, acceptance, commissioning, operation, service and retirement.

  • What is an equipment asset register?

    An asset register provides controlled identity and contextual records for equipment.

  • What is a CMMS?

    A computerized maintenance management system supports maintenance planning, work orders, history and resource coordination.

  • CMMS versus EAM versus healthcare equipment management

    CMMS emphasizes maintenance workflows, while enterprise asset management typically spans broader lifecycle and strategy functions. Healthcare equipment management adds healthcare-specific context.

  • How to develop an equipment management policy

    An equipment management policy defines governance, roles, scope, decision principles and accountability.

  • Designing an asset hierarchy

    An asset hierarchy expresses the relationship between sites, systems, equipment and maintainable components without replacing stable asset identities.

  • Equipment criticality classification

    Criticality describes the consequences of losing an asset function within an operating context.

  • Equipment responsibility matrix

    A responsibility matrix identifies who performs, approves, provides advice and is informed about a lifecycle activity.

Medical equipment management (15)

  • What is medical equipment management?

    Medical equipment management covers the administrative and technical work needed to support reliable healthcare equipment.

  • How to create a hospital equipment inventory

    An equipment inventory is a controlled list of assets, their identity and relevant operating context.

  • How to measure hospital equipment performance

    Equipment performance monitoring uses defined indicators to guide investigation and resource planning.

  • Planning medical equipment procurement

    Procurement starts with a documented operational need, not a preferred model.

  • Acceptance and commissioning of healthcare equipment

    Acceptance determines whether supplied equipment meets agreed requirements; commissioning prepares it for authorized use in its environment.

  • Classifying medical equipment for asset records

    A controlled taxonomy supports consistent grouping, not regulatory classification or clinical approval.

  • Managing hospital equipment location

    Location records describe where equipment was last recorded and when, distinct from identity and ownership.

  • Building a medical equipment service history

    Service history preserves the interventions and evidence attached to an asset throughout its lifecycle.

  • Planning hospital equipment spare parts

    Spare parts planning connects failure exposure, supplier lead time, compatibility and demand uncertainty.

  • Evaluating external equipment service providers

    Vendor selection includes capability, evidence, response expectations and confidentiality obligations.

  • Equipment pooling and temporary loans

    Equipment pooling supports shared use through transparent custody and availability rules.

  • Hospital equipment replacement planning

    Replacement planning considers lifecycle support, performance, risk, demand and economic alternatives.

  • Documenting equipment user training

    Training records support evidence of competence for specific authorized tasks.

  • Equipment incident and quarantine records

    A quarantine record separates potentially unsuitable equipment from routine availability until authorized review.

  • Reviewing a hospital equipment management program

    A program review compares documented practices, actual records and responsible roles against defined obligations.

Maintenance engineering (10)

  • How to develop a medical equipment maintenance program

    A maintenance program organizes service resources, risk priorities, tasks, records and performance review.

  • Equipment maintenance versus calibration

    Maintenance aims to preserve or restore function; calibration establishes a relationship between indications and reference values under specified conditions.

  • Choosing a maintenance strategy

    Maintenance strategy matches an asset failure context to feasible interventions and approved service requirements.

  • Designing a maintenance work order lifecycle

    A work order connects an identified need to authorized execution, verification and closure.

  • Maintenance planning versus scheduling

    Planning determines what work requires; scheduling assigns feasible time and resources.

  • Understanding a maintenance backlog

    Backlog represents identified work not yet completed under a defined inclusion rule.

  • Introduction to condition-based maintenance

    Condition-based maintenance uses observed condition information to decide when approved intervention is warranted.

  • Root cause analysis for recurring equipment problems

    Root cause analysis seeks contributing mechanisms and systemic conditions rather than accepting the first plausible explanation.

  • Maintenance documentation and evidence quality

    Maintenance evidence should make it possible for authorized people to reconstruct work and its limitations.

  • In-house versus outsourced maintenance

    Service delivery models should be assessed against capability, availability, governance and lifecycle cost.

Calibration and metrology (10)

  • Calibration fundamentals

    Calibration establishes a relationship between reference quantity values and instrument indications under stated conditions.

  • Understanding indication error

    Indication error is the difference between a recorded indication and a reference value for a defined comparison.

  • Introduction to measurement uncertainty

    Measurement uncertainty expresses dispersion attributed to a measured quantity under a defined measurement model.

  • Understanding metrological traceability

    Traceability relates a measurement result to a reference through a documented calibration chain with uncertainty contributions.

  • Reviewing calibration intervals

    Calibration intervals should be established and reviewed using justified requirements and evidence of measurement performance.

  • Reviewing calibration certificate information

    A certificate review checks whether reported information supports the intended measurement decision.

  • Calibration versus verification

    Calibration establishes a measurement relationship; verification evaluates evidence against stated requirements.

  • Handling an out-of-tolerance finding

    An out-of-tolerance finding can require evaluation of instrument use and affected prior results.

  • Structuring calibration records

    A useful calibration record links equipment identity, measurement evidence and independently defined status.

  • Calculating calibration compliance

    On-time calibration compliance counts eligible tasks completed by their deadline relative to eligible tasks due.

Reliability engineering (10)

  • How to calculate mean time between failures

    Observed MTBF for repairable assets is operating exposure divided by observed failures within a defined population and period.

  • How to calculate mean time to repair

    Observed mean time to repair is total recorded repair time divided by completed repairs.

  • Defining an equipment failure

    Reliability analysis depends on consistently defining what constitutes loss of a required function.

  • Introduction to failure mode analysis

    Failure mode analysis examines how a required function might fail and what effects follow.

  • Estimating an observed failure rate

    An observed failure rate relates counted failures to a specified exposure denominator.

  • Introduction to survival and censored observations

    Survival analysis accounts for time to an event and observations that end before the event occurs.

  • Measuring repeat breakdowns

    Repeat breakdowns describe recurring failures within a defined relationship and time window.

  • Introduction to reliability block diagrams

    A reliability block diagram represents how component functions contribute to system success under explicit assumptions.

  • Monitoring reliability improvement

    Reliability improvement tracks changes in failure behavior after documented interventions.

  • Assessing reliability data quality

    Reliable inference depends on accurate event, exposure and equipment population records.

Readiness and availability (10)

  • What is equipment readiness?

    Readiness describes whether an asset meets defined prerequisites for its intended operational use at a given time.

  • Equipment readiness versus equipment availability

    Availability measures whether equipment is available over a defined period; readiness additionally depends on prerequisites for the intended use.

  • How to calculate equipment availability

    Time-based availability is the fraction of scheduled time not spent unavailable.

  • How to calculate equipment downtime

    Downtime is the time an asset is unavailable under a defined status and measurement rule.

  • Defining equipment readiness criteria

    Readiness is assessed against explicit prerequisites for a defined task and context at a point in time.

  • Scheduled versus calendar availability

    An availability measure depends on which observation time is included in its denominator.

  • Equipment status transitions and evidence

    Status transitions describe a change in recorded operational condition with time and supporting evidence.

  • Equipment redundancy and service capacity

    Redundancy can reduce the operational consequences of loss of one asset when remaining resources meet the required function.

  • Understanding equipment restoration time

    Restoration time includes the elapsed interval from a defined interruption start to verified restoration.

  • Running an equipment readiness review

    A readiness review brings evidence and unresolved constraints together for authorized operational decisions.

Equipment management KPIs (10)

  • How to calculate preventive maintenance compliance

    On-time preventive maintenance compliance is the fraction of eligible due tasks completed by their deadline.

  • What is operational leakage?

    Trivsel uses operational leakage as a proposed organizing concept for avoidable operational losses and delays associated with physical assets.

  • Measuring fault response delay

    Response delay measures elapsed time between a reported fault and the first defined response.

  • Measuring repair backlog age

    Backlog age describes elapsed time since the defined opening event for work still unresolved at a cutoff.

  • Measuring overdue maintenance

    Overdue maintenance measures actions past their defined due dates that remain incomplete under a consistent eligibility rule.

  • Measuring overdue calibration

    Overdue calibration counts eligible calibration actions whose deadline has passed and completion remains outstanding.

  • Measuring equipment utilization

    Utilization describes used time relative to a defined available or scheduled capacity denominator.

  • Calculating undiscounted lifecycle cost

    Undiscounted lifecycle cost sums relevant outlays and subtracts residual value over a common study period.

  • Choosing meaningful KPI denominators

    A denominator identifies the population or exposure to which a numerator relates.

  • Reviewing equipment KPI dashboards

    Dashboards support decisions only when their definitions, data quality and context are explicit.

Standards and regulatory assessment (10)

  • Introduction to ISO 55001

    ISO 55001 specifies requirements for an asset management system. It is not a product feature checklist.

  • Introduction to ANSI/AAMI EQ56

    ANSI/AAMI EQ56 is a healthcare equipment management standard reference.

  • Selecting standards for an equipment management program

    Standards selection begins with scope, intended use, jurisdiction, contracts and the organization's actual activities.

  • Information security for asset management systems

    Equipment management information can be operationally sensitive even when patient data is excluded.

  • Privacy by design for equipment records

    Privacy by design limits personal information to justified purposes and embeds protections in workflows.

  • Medical software intended-use assessment

    Intended use and actual functions are central inputs to assessing medical software regulatory scope.

  • Quality management and equipment management

    Quality management connects defined processes, evidence, responsibilities and continuous improvement.

  • Evaluating a calibration laboratory scope

    Laboratory competence evidence must be considered in relation to the specific measurement service required.

  • Equipment management continuity planning

    Continuity planning identifies how essential equipment-related processes can operate during disruptions.

  • Building a compliance evidence register

    A compliance register traces requirements to owners, applicability decisions, controls and current evidence.

Practical methods and tools (10)

  • Artificial intelligence in equipment management

    AI can support pattern detection, forecasting and analysis of physical asset records, subject to data quality and validation.

  • Using an equipment inventory template

    An inventory template provides a starting structure for consistent equipment identity and context.

  • Using a preventive maintenance schedule template

    A schedule template makes eligible tasks, due dates and responsible roles visible.

  • Using an equipment risk assessment worksheet

    A worksheet structures hazards, scenarios, consequences, controls and follow-up questions.

  • Using an equipment management maturity assessment

    A maturity assessment compares evidence of practices against a declared developmental rubric.

  • Using an equipment replacement worksheet

    A replacement worksheet compares alternatives using transparent assumptions and evidence.

  • Using an operational leakage worksheet

    An operational leakage worksheet connects operational losses to clearly defined intervals and causes.

  • Using a readiness evidence worksheet

    A readiness worksheet presents prerequisite evidence and unknown or unmet conditions for review.

  • Using an equipment data quality audit worksheet

    A data quality worksheet measures whether records are sufficiently complete and consistent for a defined purpose.

  • Validating an engineering calculator

    A calculator should implement a stated formula with explicit assumptions and testable behavior.

Career development (5)

  • What does a biomedical equipment technician do?

    Biomedical equipment technicians support the technical management and service of healthcare equipment within their training and authorization.

  • How to become a healthcare technology manager

    Healthcare technology management combines technical understanding, governance, planning, people management and service performance.

  • Developing a reliability engineering learning pathway

    Reliability engineering combines failure mechanisms, statistics, engineering judgment and improvement methods.

  • Developing an asset management career pathway

    Asset management connects lifecycle decisions, engineering, governance, finance and organizational objectives.

  • Building an equipment management learning portfolio

    A portfolio demonstrates reasoning, evidence handling and communication through appropriately authorized examples.
