Professional glossary

100 linked introductory concepts for asset and equipment management.

Development preview. Editorial and legal review pending.

Definitions

equipment management
Equipment management coordinates identification, location, condition, use, maintenance, calibration and retirement of equipment. Read guide
asset management
Asset management coordinates asset-related decisions to deliver value over the lifecycle. Equipment management is one practical application. Read guide
medical equipment management
Medical equipment management covers the administrative and technical work needed to support reliable healthcare equipment. Read guide
equipment readiness
Readiness describes whether an asset meets defined prerequisites for its intended operational use at a given time. Read guide
Equipment readiness versus equipment availability
Availability measures whether equipment is available over a defined period; readiness additionally depends on prerequisites for the intended use. Read guide
Equipment maintenance versus calibration
Maintenance aims to preserve or restore function; calibration establishes a relationship between indications and reference values under specified conditions. Read guide
How to create a hospital equipment inventory
An equipment inventory is a controlled list of assets, their identity and relevant operating context. Read guide
How to develop a medical equipment maintenance program
A maintenance program organizes service resources, risk priorities, tasks, records and performance review. Read guide
How to calculate equipment downtime
Downtime is the time an asset is unavailable under a defined status and measurement rule. Read guide
How to calculate mean time between failures
Observed MTBF for repairable assets is operating exposure divided by observed failures within a defined population and period. Read guide
How to calculate mean time to repair
Observed mean time to repair is total recorded repair time divided by completed repairs. Read guide
How to calculate equipment availability
Time-based availability is the fraction of scheduled time not spent unavailable. Read guide
How to calculate preventive maintenance compliance
On-time preventive maintenance compliance is the fraction of eligible due tasks completed by their deadline. Read guide
operational leakage
Trivsel uses operational leakage as a proposed organizing concept for avoidable operational losses and delays associated with physical assets. Read guide
How to measure hospital equipment performance
Equipment performance monitoring uses defined indicators to guide investigation and resource planning. Read guide
an equipment lifecycle
The equipment lifecycle spans need definition, acquisition, acceptance, commissioning, operation, service and retirement. Read guide
an equipment asset register
An asset register provides controlled identity and contextual records for equipment. Read guide
a CMMS
A computerized maintenance management system supports maintenance planning, work orders, history and resource coordination. Read guide
CMMS versus EAM versus healthcare equipment management
CMMS emphasizes maintenance workflows, while enterprise asset management typically spans broader lifecycle and strategy functions. Healthcare equipment management adds healthcare-specific context. Read guide
How to develop an equipment management policy
An equipment management policy defines governance, roles, scope, decision principles and accountability. Read guide
Introduction to ISO 55001
ISO 55001 specifies requirements for an asset management system. It is not a product feature checklist. Read guide
Introduction to ANSI/AAMI EQ56
ANSI/AAMI EQ56 is a healthcare equipment management standard reference. Read guide
What does a biomedical equipment technician do
Biomedical equipment technicians support the technical management and service of healthcare equipment within their training and authorization. Read guide
How to become a healthcare technology manager
Healthcare technology management combines technical understanding, governance, planning, people management and service performance. Read guide
Artificial intelligence in equipment management
AI can support pattern detection, forecasting and analysis of physical asset records, subject to data quality and validation. Read guide
Designing an asset hierarchy
An asset hierarchy expresses the relationship between sites, systems, equipment and maintainable components without replacing stable asset identities. Read guide
Equipment criticality classification
Criticality describes the consequences of losing an asset function within an operating context. Read guide
Equipment responsibility matrix
A responsibility matrix identifies who performs, approves, provides advice and is informed about a lifecycle activity. Read guide
Planning medical equipment procurement
Procurement starts with a documented operational need, not a preferred model. Read guide
Acceptance and commissioning of healthcare equipment
Acceptance determines whether supplied equipment meets agreed requirements; commissioning prepares it for authorized use in its environment. Read guide
Classifying medical equipment for asset records
A controlled taxonomy supports consistent grouping, not regulatory classification or clinical approval. Read guide
Managing hospital equipment location
Location records describe where equipment was last recorded and when, distinct from identity and ownership. Read guide
Building a medical equipment service history
Service history preserves the interventions and evidence attached to an asset throughout its lifecycle. Read guide
Planning hospital equipment spare parts
Spare parts planning connects failure exposure, supplier lead time, compatibility and demand uncertainty. Read guide
Evaluating external equipment service providers
Vendor selection includes capability, evidence, response expectations and confidentiality obligations. Read guide
Equipment pooling and temporary loans
Equipment pooling supports shared use through transparent custody and availability rules. Read guide
Hospital equipment replacement planning
Replacement planning considers lifecycle support, performance, risk, demand and economic alternatives. Read guide
Documenting equipment user training
Training records support evidence of competence for specific authorized tasks. Read guide
Equipment incident and quarantine records
A quarantine record separates potentially unsuitable equipment from routine availability until authorized review. Read guide
Reviewing a hospital equipment management program
A program review compares documented practices, actual records and responsible roles against defined obligations. Read guide
Choosing a maintenance strategy
Maintenance strategy matches an asset failure context to feasible interventions and approved service requirements. Read guide
Designing a maintenance work order lifecycle
A work order connects an identified need to authorized execution, verification and closure. Read guide
Maintenance planning versus scheduling
Planning determines what work requires; scheduling assigns feasible time and resources. Read guide
Understanding a maintenance backlog
Backlog represents identified work not yet completed under a defined inclusion rule. Read guide
Introduction to condition-based maintenance
Condition-based maintenance uses observed condition information to decide when approved intervention is warranted. Read guide
Root cause analysis for recurring equipment problems
Root cause analysis seeks contributing mechanisms and systemic conditions rather than accepting the first plausible explanation. Read guide
Maintenance documentation and evidence quality
Maintenance evidence should make it possible for authorized people to reconstruct work and its limitations. Read guide
In-house versus outsourced maintenance
Service delivery models should be assessed against capability, availability, governance and lifecycle cost. Read guide
Calibration fundamentals
Calibration establishes a relationship between reference quantity values and instrument indications under stated conditions. Read guide
Understanding indication error
Indication error is the difference between a recorded indication and a reference value for a defined comparison. Read guide
Introduction to measurement uncertainty
Measurement uncertainty expresses dispersion attributed to a measured quantity under a defined measurement model. Read guide
Understanding metrological traceability
Traceability relates a measurement result to a reference through a documented calibration chain with uncertainty contributions. Read guide
Reviewing calibration intervals
Calibration intervals should be established and reviewed using justified requirements and evidence of measurement performance. Read guide
Reviewing calibration certificate information
A certificate review checks whether reported information supports the intended measurement decision. Read guide
Calibration versus verification
Calibration establishes a measurement relationship; verification evaluates evidence against stated requirements. Read guide
Handling an out-of-tolerance finding
An out-of-tolerance finding can require evaluation of instrument use and affected prior results. Read guide
Structuring calibration records
A useful calibration record links equipment identity, measurement evidence and independently defined status. Read guide
Calculating calibration compliance
On-time calibration compliance counts eligible tasks completed by their deadline relative to eligible tasks due. Read guide
Defining an equipment failure
Reliability analysis depends on consistently defining what constitutes loss of a required function. Read guide
Introduction to failure mode analysis
Failure mode analysis examines how a required function might fail and what effects follow. Read guide
Estimating an observed failure rate
An observed failure rate relates counted failures to a specified exposure denominator. Read guide
Introduction to survival and censored observations
Survival analysis accounts for time to an event and observations that end before the event occurs. Read guide
Measuring repeat breakdowns
Repeat breakdowns describe recurring failures within a defined relationship and time window. Read guide
Introduction to reliability block diagrams
A reliability block diagram represents how component functions contribute to system success under explicit assumptions. Read guide
Monitoring reliability improvement
Reliability improvement tracks changes in failure behavior after documented interventions. Read guide
Assessing reliability data quality
Reliable inference depends on accurate event, exposure and equipment population records. Read guide
Defining equipment readiness criteria
Readiness is assessed against explicit prerequisites for a defined task and context at a point in time. Read guide
Scheduled versus calendar availability
An availability measure depends on which observation time is included in its denominator. Read guide
Equipment status transitions and evidence
Status transitions describe a change in recorded operational condition with time and supporting evidence. Read guide
Equipment redundancy and service capacity
Redundancy can reduce the operational consequences of loss of one asset when remaining resources meet the required function. Read guide
Understanding equipment restoration time
Restoration time includes the elapsed interval from a defined interruption start to verified restoration. Read guide
Running an equipment readiness review
A readiness review brings evidence and unresolved constraints together for authorized operational decisions. Read guide
Measuring fault response delay
Response delay measures elapsed time between a reported fault and the first defined response. Read guide
Measuring repair backlog age
Backlog age describes elapsed time since the defined opening event for work still unresolved at a cutoff. Read guide
Measuring overdue maintenance
Overdue maintenance measures actions past their defined due dates that remain incomplete under a consistent eligibility rule. Read guide
Measuring overdue calibration
Overdue calibration counts eligible calibration actions whose deadline has passed and completion remains outstanding. Read guide
Measuring equipment utilization
Utilization describes used time relative to a defined available or scheduled capacity denominator. Read guide
Calculating undiscounted lifecycle cost
Undiscounted lifecycle cost sums relevant outlays and subtracts residual value over a common study period. Read guide
Choosing meaningful KPI denominators
A denominator identifies the population or exposure to which a numerator relates. Read guide
Reviewing equipment KPI dashboards
Dashboards support decisions only when their definitions, data quality and context are explicit. Read guide
Selecting standards for an equipment management program
Standards selection begins with scope, intended use, jurisdiction, contracts and the organization's actual activities. Read guide
Information security for asset management systems
Equipment management information can be operationally sensitive even when patient data is excluded. Read guide
Privacy by design for equipment records
Privacy by design limits personal information to justified purposes and embeds protections in workflows. Read guide
Medical software intended-use assessment
Intended use and actual functions are central inputs to assessing medical software regulatory scope. Read guide
Quality management and equipment management
Quality management connects defined processes, evidence, responsibilities and continuous improvement. Read guide
Evaluating a calibration laboratory scope
Laboratory competence evidence must be considered in relation to the specific measurement service required. Read guide
Equipment management continuity planning
Continuity planning identifies how essential equipment-related processes can operate during disruptions. Read guide
Building a compliance evidence register
A compliance register traces requirements to owners, applicability decisions, controls and current evidence. Read guide
Using an equipment inventory template
An inventory template provides a starting structure for consistent equipment identity and context. Read guide
Using a preventive maintenance schedule template
A schedule template makes eligible tasks, due dates and responsible roles visible. Read guide
Using an equipment risk assessment worksheet
A worksheet structures hazards, scenarios, consequences, controls and follow-up questions. Read guide
Using an equipment management maturity assessment
A maturity assessment compares evidence of practices against a declared developmental rubric. Read guide
Using an equipment replacement worksheet
A replacement worksheet compares alternatives using transparent assumptions and evidence. Read guide
Using an operational leakage worksheet
An operational leakage worksheet connects operational losses to clearly defined intervals and causes. Read guide
Using a readiness evidence worksheet
A readiness worksheet presents prerequisite evidence and unknown or unmet conditions for review. Read guide
Using an equipment data quality audit worksheet
A data quality worksheet measures whether records are sufficiently complete and consistent for a defined purpose. Read guide
Validating an engineering calculator
A calculator should implement a stated formula with explicit assumptions and testable behavior. Read guide
Developing a reliability engineering learning pathway
Reliability engineering combines failure mechanisms, statistics, engineering judgment and improvement methods. Read guide
Developing an asset management career pathway
Asset management connects lifecycle decisions, engineering, governance, finance and organizational objectives. Read guide
