The Problem

Where is this device?
When was it last serviced?

In most hospitals, nobody can answer quickly. Equipment records live in paper files and spreadsheets scattered across departments — and downtime is often discovered only at the moment it matters most.

Before Trivsel

Paper logbooks. Spreadsheets no one updates. Calibration dates that slip. Faults discovered at the bedside instead of the dashboard. A biomedical engineer spending more time searching for records than fixing equipment.

With Trivsel

One QR code per device. Scan it and see the current status, calibration schedule, and complete fault history. Report a problem in seconds from any phone — no login required. Engineers track everything from one dashboard.

How It Works

Four steps to full visibility.

Setup takes one visit. From that point forward, every device tells its own story.

01 — Register
Device goes into the systemDepartment, manufacturer, model, serial number, and calibration schedule — entered once during onboarding.
02 — Label
QR code affixed to the deviceEach device gets a unique code that links directly to its full record. Printed and stuck on during the same visit.
03 — Scan
Any phone, any timeA nurse, doctor, or patient family member scans the code and sees the device's status and history. Reporting a fault takes 30 seconds and requires no account.
04 — Track
Engineers see everythingThe dashboard shows every open fault, upcoming calibration, and device status across all departments. Reminders send automatically.

About

Built by someone who's lived the problem.

Trivsel was founded by Nisong Monyimba, a biomedical engineer and applied mathematician with a B.Sc. Biomedical Engineering from KNUST and an M.Sc. Biomedical Engineering from Arizona State University.

The idea started at Komfo Anokye Teaching Hospital in Kumasi, where Nisong worked hands-on with medical equipment maintenance and calibration, documenting device performance, and watching how much of that work depended on paper records instead of a system built for the job.

That understanding deepened through later work in medical device manufacturing and quality engineering at Abbott Laboratories and Vastek Inc., using failure analysis and risk modeling to understand exactly how equipment problems compound when they go untracked, and through research into medical device regulation across 20 African countries, which shaped how Trivsel was built for the realities those systems actually face.

Trivsel is built specifically for those realities: staff on ordinary phones, variable connectivity, and biomedical teams stretched across large device inventories. No special hardware. No expensive subscriptions.
